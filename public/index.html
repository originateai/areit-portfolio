Cash on cash — actual money received, not accounting yield
Loading…
Two things a yield number hides.

Cover. If AFFO cover is below 1.0x the distribution is not funded by the property's cash earnings — the difference is coming from debt, asset sales or capital. That is the balance sheet paying you, and it cannot continue indefinitely.

Return of capital. The tax-deferred slice of an A-REIT distribution reduces your cost base. It is cash in hand, but it is partly your own money coming back, and it converts into a capital gain on sale. Over a retirement horizon that distinction compounds — a 7% distribution that is 30% tax-deferred is not a 7% return on capital.
Read this before comparing rows. Property yield is calculated on equity and is therefore leveraged — it is comparable to a listed yield as cash return, but not as risk. Tax figures are a modelling estimate, not tax advice, and component splits are assumed from asset class until an actual annual tax statement is loaded.

Manage Holdings

Edit, reclassify, close or delete a position · asset class drives both the tax profile and the valuation method

Asset class is not cosmetic. It selects the default tax profile — a credit holding is assumed 100% unfranked interest and taxed at your full marginal rate, while lic is assumed franked. It also selects which valuation methods apply. Misclassify a position and its post-tax yield will be wrong in a way that looks entirely plausible.

Rows sourced from trades are rebuilt from the ledger whenever you press Refresh from trades. To hand-edit one permanently, switch its source to manual — sync then leaves it alone.

Levered DCF — free cash flow to equity, discounted
—
FCFE, not distributions. This discounts all cash available to equity — AFFO plus the debt drawn as the book revalues — where the DDM discounts only what is paid out. The gap between the two is the value of what management retains and of gearing into a revaluing book. The terminal is the exit-cap equity value, so the DCF is anchored to a property valuation at exit rather than to a perpetuity of its own cash flows.
The statements articulate. Balance sheet balances, cash flow ties to the cash movement, and equity rolls with profit less distributions — checked every rebuild, with the result shown next to the hedging toggle in section 2. If it ever reads does not articulate, these numbers are not trustworthy and the failing check is listed.

The hurdle is a joint test, measured gross. 12% IRR on a 3% yield is a growth stock, not passive income; 7% yield on a 4% IRR is capital erosion dressed as income. A row passes only when it clears both. Both are judged pre-tax, because an asset's return does not change with who owns it — the post-tax column beside them is the overlay, and it is what separates two names that clear the hurdle equally. Where a valuation input is missing the cell reads — rather than a guess.

Check the Basis column before trusting a discount. Only REITs with a built workbook have the cap-rate, discount-rate and multiple assumptions the other three methods need. Without them the blend collapses to book NTA alone, which is the directors' cap rates rather than the market's — those rows are greyed and produce the largest, least reliable discounts on the page.

This engine does not agree with the workbooks, by design. Excel's blended value is its equity DCF (they are identical in every stored row); this engine blends four methods and weights NTA at 15%, which sits above DCF for every REIT here. The engine therefore reads 5–17% higher on the five modelled names. That is a methodology difference, not a reconciliation error — but it means the two numbers are not interchangeable, and the workbook remains the more conservative of the pair.
